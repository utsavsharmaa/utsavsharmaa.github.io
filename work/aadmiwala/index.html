OVERVIEW

Hiring blue-collar staff through trusted local agencies

Aadmiwala was a live project for a start-up in Patna. They wanted one place where a business could hire blue-collar staff through trusted local agencies.

​

In about a month, I took it from research to a live website and dashboards for three kinds of users.

The client

Aadmiwala is a Patna start-up that fills blue-collar jobs like security, housekeeping, office support, cooks and electricians. It links employers with verified local agencies, called HUBs, and the HUBs supply the workers.

My role

End-to-end product design in about a month: research, structure, flows, visual design, components and prototype. Phase 1 was the full platform, with a dashboard for every role. Phase 2 was a launch website.

What shipped

80+ screens across two phases · 3 role dashboards · 8 prototype flows · brand guidelines · a site that ran live for about a month.

THE BRIEF

The service had to carry one hire across three sides.

Aadmiwala connected businesses with local staffing agencies (HUBs), then with the workers they placed. I shaped the service and interfaces; a short timeline and build budget determined what launched first.

THE SYSTEM

The hardest design problem was the handoff.

A business requests staff, a local HUB sources candidates, and a worker needs to know who is responsible and what happens next. The system has to preserve that context across the chain.

BUSINESS

Needs a dependable way to hire

Post a role, assess staffing partners, and see where hiring stands.

RECRUITING HUB

Needs clear work and ownership

See routed requests, send candidates, and follow placement status.

JOB SEEKER

Needs trust and a clear next step

Know who supplies the role, whether fees apply, and what happens next.

THE PRODUCT PROMISE

Business request → HUB sourcing → candidate handoff → worker status and next step

These are design needs inferred from the brief and screens, not direct interview findings. No worker interviews were conducted within the short timeline.

DESIGN SYSTEM

One system, every screen

The public site, sign-up and three role-based dashboards use the same visual rules and connected flows, so the product feels like one service from first visit through hiring.

Client brief

The client shared the service model, business needs and two reference sites: CBREX and Workmate.

Build constraints

The developer’s estimate put the full platform beyond budget, so I planned a phased release.

Research and assumptions

With no user interviews, I used secondary research to guide assumptions about phone-led hiring and the support people may need with forms and documents.

The system connected the same hiring process across three roles, while the phased plan kept the launch within budget.

PROJECT

From full platform to phased launch

I designed the full three-role platform first. After the developer estimated it would exceed budget, I simplified the launch to a public website, HUB onboarding and email requests. The client received both versions.

PROBLEM

Hiring breaks at every handoff

I followed one hire from the business to the agency to the worker. Each side got stuck at the same point: passing the job along.

Employers

A company with sites in Patna, Delhi and Noida needs a different agency, contract and invoice in each city. And it can't compare quality before signing.

Recruiting HUBs

Good local agencies are just one-line listings on IndiaMART or Justdial. They win work on price, not on their track record.

Job seekers

Fake job posts, middlemen who charge fees, and no reply after applying. Most never find out what happened to their application.

How might we give a business one trusted partner for staff in any city, while keeping local agencies in work and workers safe?

Sources: Deloitte India, Blue-Collar Workforce Trends 2025; WorkIndia salary report via CB Insights (Mar 2026).

HOW AADMIWALA WORKS

How it works: one hire, start to finish

Ramesh starts a verified job near home. Arjun pays one invoice to Aadmiwala, and Rakesh gets paid on time.

Who does what

Employers ask

Post one request, accept a quote, pick from checked staff at your site, and pay one invoice. No agency hunting city by city.

Aadmiwala + HUBs do the work

Find the people, call them, sign them up, train them and keep every side updated on where the hire stands.

Workers answer a call

No app or form needed. A verified job near home, PF/ESI and on-time pay, with someone helping at every step.

Aadmiwala doesn't just list jobs. It owns the whole hire, so each side only does what it's good at.

CLIENT REFERENCES

Two sites the client pointed me to

Before I designed anything, the client sent me two websites and said, "it's kind of what we are looking for." I studied both to see who they serve, how they make money and what visitors are asked to do.

CBREX

cbr.exchange

WHAT IT DOES

A hiring platform for businesses. A company posts a role, and CBREX sends it to the right firms out of 4,000+ recruiting agencies, all under one contract. The company pays only when it hires. Agencies join free and earn a fee per hire.

WHAT I TOOK FROM IT

What I took: one platform between businesses and many agencies, with one contract and one invoice. Agencies get their own page and sign-up, and every page has an FAQ and "Book a Demo".

Workmate

workmate.asia

WHAT IT DOES

A platform for blue-collar work in Indonesia and Thailand. It matched pre-screened workers with nearby businesses and handled contracts, attendance and pay, with no agencies in between.

WHAT I TOOK FROM IT

What I took: a focus on blue-collar workers, with verification and protection for them. The difference: Aadmiwala keeps the local agencies and adds the worker as a third user. That's where "one job, three sides" comes from.

MARKET RESEARCH

Research: who else is doing this, and how?

I looked at who else solves blue-collar hiring and what gap is left.

Job apps

Apna, WorkIndia. Workers apply, then the employer screens and manages every hire

Staffing firms

Quess, TeamLease. Workers on their payroll, on long contracts with one agency

Workforce SaaS

BetterPlace. Full hiring lifecycle, built and priced for large enterprises

Local agencies

Found on IndiaMART, Justdial. Calls and WhatsApp leads, unverified

What this meant: each one solves part of the hire, but businesses still have to find, check and manage agencies one by one.

Sources: apna.co; WorkIndia employer guide (Internshala); betterplace.co.in; Quess Corp company profile; IndiaMART agency listings.

DECISION STORY 01

Make “Request a call” the main way in for job seekers

My first job seeker screens assumed workers would sign themselves up with an OTP. I changed that.

HOW THE DECISION UNFOLDED

01

Before: Sign-up said “Just your number and a few details” and ended in “Get OTP”. Job cards had Call and Apply. The old screens are kept as a backup.

02

Options: Self sign-up with an OTP for everyone, or an assisted route where someone calls the worker.

03

Why: Most workers never sign up themselves. Aadmiwala staff or a HUB call them, train them and add their documents, because many aren’t comfortable with forms or apps. I worked this out from who these workers are and the jobs they apply for. People with more education usually go for other kinds of jobs.

04

What I did next: “Request a call” became the main button: “Share your number and our team will call you, train you and do the paperwork.” OTP sign-up moved to a link. “Apply” became “I’m interested”, and the app became an extra feature.

THE TRADE-OFF

Help first, app second.

More work for Aadmiwala and HUB staff, who now make the calls and do the paperwork. (my reading)

Workers who can use a phone still get the app, with “Call” beside “I’m interested”.

Based on how hiring works for these workers, from client talks. Not tested.

“Request a call” is the main way in. OTP sign-up is a link.

DECISION STORY 02

Cut a three-dashboard platform down to a website

The developer said Phase 1 would cost far more than the client’s budget. I had to decide what Phase 2 could live without.

HOW THE DECISION UNFOLDED

01

Before: Phase 1 gave employers, HUBs and job seekers their own login and dashboard, all built around one job post.

02

Options: I considered keeping one dashboard, just for Aadmiwala to manage everything. The budget didn’t allow it, so the client asked for something very simple: requests arrive by email or a Google Form.

03

Why: Agencies are Aadmiwala’s partners. Aadmiwala needs proper information about them, and so do the employers who recruit through them. That’s why agencies got a full sign-up.

04

What I did next: Kept: the public website, a requirement form that promises a call back within 2 hours, and agency sign-up, login, profile and services. Cut: employer and job seeker accounts, all dashboards, landing pages, blog and demo booking.

05

Result: Phases 1 and 2 both went to the client. The website ran live for about a month.

THE TRADE-OFF

A simple website. No dashboards.

Without dashboards, nobody could track a hire on the platform. Requests arrived by email and Aadmiwala called back.

What carried over from Phase 1: agency profiles with scores, agency sign-up and login, and the requirement form.

Phase 2: businesses send a requirement through a simple form.

REFLECTION

What I learned, and what I'd design next

The biggest lessons weren't about visuals. They were about naming things once, designing for every user, and making every number answer "compared to what?"

Name each user once

The first files called the agency five different things. One name per role (Employer, Recruiting HUB, Job seeker) keeps copy, flows and data in line.

Design both sides of a marketplace

Phase 2 gave agencies accounts while businesses only had forms. Phase 1 gave every role its own dashboard.

Dashboards should drive a decision

Every number now answers "compared to what?" and every list ends in an action.

Wire the prototype end to end

Most links in the shipped prototype had no destination. Wiring eight end-to-end flows exposed the missing screens, so Phase 2 now also covers errors, empty states, emails and mobile.
