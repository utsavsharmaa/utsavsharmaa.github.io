Brand Identity & Packaging

Carved in stone. Told in shadow.

“KASHIKRITI” is a branding and packaging project centred on the traditional soft-stone jaali craft of Varanasi. The project explores how a craft rooted in material, skill and architectural heritage can be communicated through a contemporary visual identity, packaging and storytelling.

The craft

A city's craft, carved from a single stone.

Varanasi's soft stone jaali work dates back to the 7th century BC. Artisans in Ramnagar, Luxa and Sarnath carve soapstone into lamps, animals and screens, without a single joint. The craft received its GI certificate in 2018.

How it is made

Five steps from block to jaali.

The stone is marked and cut, then roughly shaped. Hundreds of tiny holes are drilled, removing stone bit by bit. The piece is smoothed and polished. In undercut work, a smaller elephant is carved inside a larger one, from the same stone.

The problem

Recognized on paper. Unseen by its own city.

Once carved for temples, forts and the kings of Kashi, soft stone jaali has lost its patrons. From around 3,500 artisans recorded in its GI filing, only a few hundred still carve today. Pieces sell for under ₹1,200, often through middlemen, beside machine-made copies.

Why it is dying

Middlemen paying artisans a fraction of a fair price.

Lost patronage: royal patrons of Kashi once sustained the Ramnagar carvers.

Slow production with no modern infrastructure, and copies entering the market.

Young people leaving for better-paid work.

What this means for Kashikriti

01

No one owns the craft.

Every seller lists jaali beside dozens of other crafts. No brand speaks for Varanasi jaali alone.

02

Story sets the price, not stone.

Similar pieces sell from ₹176 to over ₹40,000. The higher prices come with a named maker and a story.

The buyer never meets the craft.

Most listings give only the GI label, size and price. The carving, the undercut and the artisan stay unseen.

Brand strategy

Brand Vision

To revive and reimagine stone jali works as a timeless expression of form, and cultural depth, creating meaningful experiences while preserving the essence of Banaras craftsmanship.

Brand Culture

Naming

Ten names from Hindi roots. One that means “the form of Kashi.”

I built names in two directions. One joined Kashi with craft words: Kashikriti, Kashitva, Kashikraft, Kashikar. The other joined shadow (saaya) with light, story and hand: Saayakaar, Saayakatha, Saayank. Three names went further, each tested as a mini identity with its own tagline. Kashikriti won because it joins the place, Kashi, with the made thing, kriti.

Digitised Logo

Nine marks, three names, one test. I digitised the strongest sketches and set each one with a shortlisted name: Kashikriti, Kashitva and Kashikraft. Seeing mark and wordmark together showed which pairs held up, and two directions stood out: the jharokha arch and the four-lobed knot.

Digitised Logo

Nine marks, three names, one test. I digitised the strongest sketches and set each one with a shortlisted name: Kashikriti, Kashitva and Kashikraft. Seeing mark and wordmark together showed which pairs held up, and two directions stood out: the jharokha arch and the four-lobed knot.

Direction 01

The arch: क, carved like a jaali. It is built from three things the craft already holds: a calligraphic क for Kashi and kriti, the radiating floral carving of a real soapstone piece, and the jharokha windows of Banaras. Together they make a mark you look through, the way light passes through a jaali. This became the final logo.

Direction 01

The arch: क, carved like a jaali. It is built from three things the craft already holds: a calligraphic क for Kashi and kriti, the radiating floral carving of a real soapstone piece, and the jharokha windows of Banaras. Together they make a mark you look through, the way light passes through a jaali. This became the final logo.

Direction 02

The knot: four lobes, one craft. क and K are drawn as one continuous line and looped into a knot, its lobes reading as the artisan’s hand, a community working together and the round jaali disc. It was striking, but it did not speak of the jaali directly, so the arch went forward.
